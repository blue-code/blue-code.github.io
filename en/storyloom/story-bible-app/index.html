Story Bible App: What to Look For and Which to Try

Keep your characters, relationships, and plot in one place you can actually see

A good story bible app keeps your characters, their relationships, and your plot events together, lets you see the whole structure at once, and lets you export everything so your work never gets stuck in one app. One thing to know before you search: typing "story bible" into the App Store also returns a lot of scripture apps. Check that a result is actually built for fiction writing before you download it.

What a story bible app should cover

  • Characters: role, a short summary, backstory, and traits
  • Relationships: who is connected to whom, and how
  • Timeline: events in order
  • Branches: alternate routes or endings, if your story has them
  • Export: a backup you can open in other tools

How StoryLoom organizes a story

StoryLoom is an on-device story bible for novels, screenplays, web fiction, webtoon stories, TTRPG campaigns, and fanfic. Characters are nodes, relationships are threads, events form a timeline, and branches are routes.

  • Projects: one per world, each with a title, logline, genre, and cover color
  • Characters: start with a name, role, and one-liner, then add backstory and trait tags
  • Relationship map: drag nodes into place and connect them with curved threads. Each thread gets a type (family, romance, friendship, alliance, rivalry, hostility, mentor), a direction, and an optional −100 to +100 affection score that sets its color.
  • Timeline & branches: stack events in order and split them into routes for multi-ending stories
  • Export: turn a whole project into a Markdown or JSON story bible in one tap

It runs on iPhone, iPad, and Mac, and gives you a 3-column layout on larger screens. Your work is saved on your device by default. You can turn on iCloud Sync to keep your devices in step. There's no account, no ads, and no tracking. On first launch you get a sample world, so you can see how a filled-in map looks.

Quick checklist before you commit

  1. Count your main cast. If it fits in 15 per project, the free tier may be all you need.
  2. Decide whether you need branches. One route covers a linear story.
  3. Test export early so you know your notes can leave the app.
  4. Confirm the app runs on the devices you actually write on.
