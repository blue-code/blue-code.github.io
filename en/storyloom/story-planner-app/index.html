Choosing a Story Planner App: Options and What to Look For

How to pick a planning app when your cast grows and your plot starts to branch

A story planner app should do one thing well: show you the whole shape of your story so you stop losing track of it. If your problem is a growing cast, tangled relationships, or a plot with more than one ending, try StoryLoom: Novel Story Bible. It gives you a relationship map you arrange by dragging, a timeline that can branch, and Markdown export of a whole project in one tap. It's free to start on iPhone, iPad and Mac. If what you mostly need is a place to draft chapters, a writing-focused app from the list below may suit you better.

What to look for in a story planner

  • A view of the whole story. Can you see every character and plot thread on one screen, or only one note at a time?
  • Character depth. Room for a role, a backstory and traits, without having to fill everything in on day one.
  • Timeline and branches. This matters a lot for interactive fiction, TTRPG campaigns, or stories with more than one ending.
  • Export. Your story bible should be easy to take out of the app as plain files.
  • Privacy and sync. Find out where your work is stored and whether it needs an account.
  • Pricing model. Free, a one-time purchase, or a subscription.

Where StoryLoom fits

A relationship map instead of a table

Characters are nodes and relationships are curved threads between them. Each relationship has a type (family, romance, friendship, alliance, rivalry, hostility, mentor), a direction, and an optional affection score from −100 to +100. The thread's color shows what kind of bond it is. You drag nodes to arrange them, pinch to zoom, and tap any node or thread to edit it in place.

A timeline that branches

Put events in order, drag to rearrange them, and split them into branch routes for stories with more than one ending or a non-linear structure.

Export and privacy

One tap turns a whole project into a Markdown or JSON story bible. Your work stays on your device unless you turn on iCloud Sync, which uses Apple's private iCloud database to keep your iPhone, iPad and Mac in step. There's no account, no StoryLoom server, no ads and no tracking. You'll find more on the StoryLoom page.

Who it's for, and who it isn't

  • A good fit: novelists with big casts, web fiction and webtoon writers, screenwriters, fanfic writers, and TTRPG game masters keeping track of factions.
  • Probably not: anyone who wants a full manuscript editor. StoryLoom is for planning and keeping a story bible, not for drafting chapters.

Plan your story in 15 minutes

  1. Open the sample world to see how a finished map looks.
  2. Create a project with a title, a logline and a genre.
  3. Add your five most important characters with a role and a one-liner each.
  4. Connect them on the map and give each thread a type.
  5. Add the main events to the timeline, then branch where the story splits.
  6. Export to Markdown so you have a backup.
